# Science summary

From two real SPHEREx exposures six months apart we measured Neptune's heliocentric distance as 29.95 ± 0.00 AU (median of 265 exposure pairs), predicting positions from one measured position, its time and a trial distance only — no ephemeris of the target.
JPL Horizons gives 29.89 AU (+0.2 %); the same method recovers Uranus at 19.39 AU versus 19.50 AU (−0.5 %), while ignoring orbital motion gives errors from −23 % to +50 % with a season-dependent sign.
The method assumes a circular orbit near the ecliptic plane and needs the object to be visible in each frame; Neptune validates the geometry, not the sensitivity needed for a faint Planet Nine.

## Limitations and uncertainty

- Neptune proves geometry, not sensitivity: Neptune is bright and probably saturated.
- The geometry model assumes a circular prograde orbit in the ecliptic plane. Inclined or eccentric orbits can have larger prediction errors. In a standalone geometry calculation, I used a 30 AU circular orbit inclined by 20°, set its ascending-node direction along the initial heliocentric radial projection, and propagated the same June→December 2025 baseline (178.253 days) from the measured first-epoch position. At 30 AU, the inclined position differs from the ecliptic model by 1,340.38 arcsec. Fitting that inclined synthetic position with the circular ecliptic model on the 20–60 AU, 4,000-point grid returns 29.1523 AU: a −0.8477 AU (−2.83%) bias. This is one orientation-specific example, not a universal inclination correction.
- The single-pair dial-table frames use different wavelengths: 0.968 µm in June and 1.259 µm in December. Across all 58 good Neptune all-pairs exposures, measured-to-Horizons offsets span 0.141–27.418 arcsec (median 2.700 arcsec), or 0.023–4.458 SPHEREx pixels (median 0.439 px at 6.15 arcsec/px). The maximum offset divided by the 2.642 arcsec/AU local distance sensitivity gives a conservative empirical positional distance scale of 10.38 AU. These offsets include all centroid and ephemeris residuals; they do not isolate the wavelength-dependent component, so the chromatic effect itself remains unmeasured.
- Uncertainty for the 265-pair headline: bootstrap 68% interval for the median is [29.9525, 29.9525] AU, i.e. ±0.00 AU at the shown precision. Pair scatter is separate: fitted distances have std 0.0182 AU and p16–p84 29.9325–29.9625 AU. The centroid-offset propagation gives ±2.22 AU using RMS offset 5.852 arcsec divided by local sensitivity 2.642 arcsec/AU. The bootstrap interval is degenerate because many pair fits share the same grid-valued distances; it does not include centroid systematics.
- The full real-data dial over the whole distance range was not completed. E4 v2 difference stamps retained star residuals and most grid points lacked suitable coverage/matched stars/templates, so the real-data result uses the two-epoch astrometric fit instead.
